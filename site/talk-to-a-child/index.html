How to talk to a child on Oiê

A child you know uses Oiê on a tablet. They write through their mum’s or dad’s WhatsApp, and you talk with them in your usual WhatsApp, on that same number.

1. Be on the list

Only the people the mum or dad put on Oiê’s list can talk to the child. If you aren’t on it, what you send stays in the parent’s WhatsApp and never reaches the tablet. If you’re not sure, ask.

2. Start with the name

To start a conversation, like an invitation or a good night, write to the mum’s or dad’s number and start with the child’s name. A hello before the name is fine. The same goes for groups.

Reaches the tablet

  • Tito, it’s Lucas’s birthday on Saturday. Want to come?
  • Hi Tito, good night. Sleep well.
  • Good morning, Tito. How was the test?

Stays with the parent

  • Is Tito free on Saturday?
  • Tell Tito I said hi.
  • How is Tito doing?

When you talk about the child, the message is for the parents. With an invitation, they see it too and can sort out the details with you.

3. Then just talk

  • When they write, you answer

    After the child sends a message, your answers reach them for 24 hours, no name needed. After that, start with the name again.

  • A reply always reaches them

    Swipe one of their messages to the side and answer: that reaches them at any time.

  • If the mum or dad joins in

    What comes next stays with the parent, until the child writes again. Starting with the name or replying to one of their messages still reaches them.

Good to know

  • The parent sees everything

    It’s their WhatsApp: everything you send the child is on their phone too. And what you send only to them never shows on the tablet.

  • How the child’s messages arrive

    From the mum’s or dad’s number, with the child’s name in bold in front, and the emoji they picked.

  • A chat that’s the child’s own

    The parent can mark a chat as the child’s own, like a school friend’s or the cousins’ group. Then everything you send reaches them, no name needed.

  • What you can send

    Text, voice, photos, videos, GIFs and stickers. Documents, locations and polls only open in WhatsApp; the child sees a note.

  • Quiet hours

    During school or bedtime, what you send reaches the tablet with no sound. When quiet hours end, one notification says how many arrived.
